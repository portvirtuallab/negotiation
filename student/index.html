Reference

PVL Glossary

Every logistics and ERP term used across Open Lab and Click & Cargo, in one searchable place.

A ▾
ADD (Adjustment Policy), Cost element added to the invoice value to reach the customs border value under the selected Incoterm.
ADR, European Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road.
Air Export Shipment, Operational record created in the ERP to manage and execute an air export shipment.
Air Freight Rate, Price per kilogram applied to the chargeable weight in air transport.
Air Waybill, Non-negotiable transport document issued for air transportation; called a house air waybill when issued by a freight forwarder or consolidator.
AMS (Automated Manifest System), U.S. advance cargo declaration system requiring electronic manifest submission before arrival.
Arrival Notice, Official notification informing the consignee that cargo has arrived at destination.
API, A set of rules and protocols enabling interaction between the Click & Cargo ERP and other application systems.
Agreement, A final understanding reached by two or more parties on terms, price, or scope.
Agenda, A list of topics to be discussed in a meeting or negotiation.
Alliance, A strategic partnership between companies to achieve shared goals.
Anchor, The first offer or reference point that influences the rest of a negotiation.
Authority, The decision-making power to approve, reject, or modify a proposal.
B ▾
BAF (Bunker Adjustment Factor), Fuel surcharge applied to ocean freight to reflect fuel price fluctuations.
Bill of Lading (B/L), Transport document acknowledging receipt of cargo; certifies loading (export) or unloading (import).
Bill of Lading Charge, Fee applied per issued Bill of Lading document.
Border Value, Value of goods at the EU border used to assess customs duties.
BATNA, Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement; the best option available if no deal is reached.
Benchmark, A reference used to compare prices, performance, or processes.
Break-even Point, The sales level at which revenue equals total costs.
Budget, The financial limit available for a project, purchase, or operation.
Broker, An intermediary who facilitates transactions between buyer and seller.
C ▾
CAF (Currency Adjustment Factor), Surcharge applied due to exchange rate fluctuations affecting ocean freight.
Cargo Insurance, Coverage typically calculated at 110% of the commercial invoice value.
Cargo Type, Classification of cargo category (general cargo, dangerous goods, reefer, etc.).
Carrier, Individual or legal entity transporting goods.
Case ID, Internal reference assigned to a customs declaration in the H1 simulator.
Chargeable Weight, Higher of Gross Weight or Volumetric Weight, used for air freight calculation.
CIF, Incoterm where freight and insurance are included in the invoice value up to the destination port.
CIP, Incoterm where the seller pays transport and insurance to a named destination; used as customs value target in PVL.
CMR, Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage by Road.
Collect, Transport charges payable by the consignee.
Commercial Offer, Structured quotation generated in the ERP.
Consignee, Named person/entity with the right to claim the merchandise at destination.
Container Transport Order, Instruction to arrange inland movement of a container after port discharge.
Customs Clearance, Process of declaring goods to customs authorities.
Customs Value, Value at the EU border used to calculate duties. Formula: CIP = Invoice Value + Net Adjustment.
Cash Flow, The movement of money into and out of a business.
Closing, The stage where a sale or agreement is finalized.
Commission, A variable payment linked to the result of a sale or transaction.
Competitive Advantage, A factor that makes a company more attractive than its competitors.
Contract, A legally binding agreement that defines rights and obligations.
Counteroffer, A response to an initial offer with revised terms.
Customer Acquisition, The process of winning new clients.
D ▾
DDP, Incoterm where the seller assumes almost all responsibilities, including destination costs.
Declarant, Customs representative filing the declaration on behalf of the importer.
Destination Free Days, Days allowed at destination terminal before storage/demurrage charges apply.
Documentation Fee, Administrative fee related to issuing transport documentation.
Door-to-Door / Door-to-Port, Transport service scope from seller's premises to buyer's premises or destination port.
Duty % (Tariff Rate), Percentage applied to customs value according to HS Code classification.
Deal, A commercial transaction or closed agreement.
Demand, The market need or desire for a product or service.
Discount, A reduction applied to the original price.
Due Diligence, A review of facts, risks, and conditions before completing a transaction.
Distribution, The process of delivering products from the company to the customer.
E ▾
EORI, EU identification number required for customs operations.
ERP, Software used to manage logistics operations (e.g., Click & Cargo).
ETS, EU carbon Emission Trading System impacting maritime freight pricing.
ETA / ETD, Estimated Time of Arrival / Departure of vessel or aircraft.
EUR-1, Certifies origin of goods for preferential tariff treatment under trade agreements.
EXW (Ex Works), Incoterm where the buyer assumes most responsibilities from the seller's premises.
Ecosystem, The network of partners, customers, suppliers, and tools around a business.
Equity, Ownership value in a company after liabilities are deducted.
Escalation, A rise in conflict, urgency, or decision level.
Execution, The act of carrying out a business plan or agreement.
Expansion, Growth into new markets, products, or channels.
F ▾
FCL, Full Container Load, used exclusively by one shipper.
Flow, Direction of cargo movement (Export / Import).
Freight, International main carriage cost.
Freight Forwarder, Intermediary managing logistics and transportation of cargo.
Fidelity, The ability to retain customers over time.
Forecast, A projection of future sales, revenue, or demand.
Franchise, A business model that allows others to operate under an established brand.
Free Cash Flow, Cash remaining after operating and investment expenses.
Fulfilment, The complete process of receiving, preparing, and delivering an order.
G ▾
Goods Delivery, Final stage of import where cargo is delivered to consignee.
Gross Weight, Total weight of goods including packaging.
Gain, The positive financial result obtained from a transaction.
Guarantee, A promise that a product, service, or obligation will be fulfilled.
Growth, An increase in revenue, customers, or market share.
Go-to-Market, The plan used to launch and sell a product or service.
Governance, The rules and oversight that guide business decisions.
H ▾
H1 Declaration, EU customs declaration for release for free circulation (simulated in PVL).
H1 Import Simulator Pro, Educational tool to simulate EU customs declarations.
HS Code, Harmonized System classification number used to determine duty rates.
Handover, The transfer of responsibility from one party to another.
Hedge, An action taken to reduce financial or commercial risk.
Hierarchy, The structure of authority and decision-making inside an organization.
Hub, A central point for operations, logistics, or commercial activity.
Holding, A parent company that owns controlling interests in other firms.
I ▾
IATA, International Air Transport Association.
IMO Surcharge, Fee applied to dangerous goods shipments under IMO rules.
Incoterms, 11 international standard trade terms (latest: Incoterms 2020) dividing costs/risks between seller and buyer.
Inland Transport, Pre-carriage (before main carriage) or on-carriage (after arrival).
Insurance, Coverage against loss/damage, ~110% of invoice value; simulated via Play TT Circle in PVL.
Invoice Value, Commercial invoice total under selected Incoterm.
Incentive, A reward or benefit used to encourage a desired action.
Income, Money earned through sales, services, or investments.
Indexation, Adjusting a value according to an index, such as inflation or exchange rates.
Innovation, The introduction of new ideas, products, or methods.
Investment, Resources allocated today to generate future returns.
J ▾
Joint Venture, A business arrangement in which two parties share a project, risk, and return.
Justification, The business reason supporting a decision or proposal.
K ▾
Key Account, A strategically important customer managed with special attention.
KPI, Key Performance Indicator; a metric used to measure business performance.
Know-how, Practical knowledge and expertise accumulated by a company or team.
L ▾
LCL, Less than Container Load; shared container space.
Logistics Management, Administrative coordination fee covering shipment management.
Lead, A potential customer with commercial interest.
Leverage, An advantage or bargaining power used to influence an outcome.
Liquidity, The ability of a business to meet short-term obligations.
Logistics, The planning and management of flow, storage, and delivery.
LTV, Lifetime Value; the total expected value a customer generates over time.
M ▾
Main Transport Costs, Freight and insurance costs of international carriage.
Master Air Waybill / Master Bill of Lading, Issued by the airline / shipping line (main carrier).
MRN, Movement Reference Number; unique tracking number for customs declarations.
MTO, Multimodal Transport Operator, responsible for the entire transport chain.
Margin, The difference between revenue and cost.
Market Share, The portion of total market sales captured by a company.
Mediation, A process where a neutral third party helps parties reach an agreement.
Merger, The combination of two companies into one business entity.
Model, The way a business creates, delivers, and captures value.
N ▾
Net Adjustment, Total amount added/subtracted from invoice value to reach customs border value.
Net Weight, Weight of goods excluding packaging.
Notify Party, The person a carrier notifies of a shipment's arrival.
NVOCC, Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier; consolidates shipments without owning ships.
Negotiation, A process to reach an agreement between parties with different interests.
Net Profit, Profit remaining after all costs, taxes, and expenses.
Niche, A focused market segment with specific needs.
Networking, Building professional relationships that can generate business opportunities.
O ▾
Ocean Freight, Base maritime freight cost excluding surcharges.
Origin Costs, Terminal, inland and customs charges incurred before export departure.
Opportunity, A possible sale or project with commercial potential.
Offer, A commercial proposal with price and conditions.
Operating Model, The way an organization runs its processes and resources.
Outcome, The result of a business decision, action, or negotiation.
Outsourcing, Delegating a task or function to an external provider.
P ▾
Packages, Number of handling units included in shipment.
Plug-in (Reefer), Electrical connection fee for refrigerated containers.
POL / POD, Port of Loading / Port of Discharge.
Port Taxes, Official port authority charges.
Pre-Alert, Advance notification sent before cargo arrival with documentation.
Prepaid, Freight charges paid by the shipper.
Pricing Structure, Method used to calculate freight/surcharges (per container, ton, day, %, document).
Pipeline, The stage-by-stage flow of sales opportunities.
Position, The formal stance a party takes during a negotiation.
Portfolio, The collection of products, services, or clients managed by a company.
Profitability, The capacity to generate profit.
Proposal, A formal offer presented to a client or partner.
Prospection, The search for new clients or opportunities.
R ▾
Rate Sheet, Structured table of freight rates and surcharge conditions.
Ro-Ro, Roll-on/Roll-off shipping for wheeled cargo.
Rate, A price, fee, or percentage applied to a business transaction.
Return on Investment, A measure of profit relative to the amount invested.
Revenue, Income generated by sales or services.
Risk, The possibility of loss, delay, or failure.
Retention, The ability to keep customers active over time.
S ▾
SAD, Single Administrative Document; EU customs declaration for exports outside the EU.
Sales Agent, Representative selling on behalf of exporter/importer.
Shipper, Party contracting carriage and dispatching goods.
Surcharges, Additional mandatory charges (BAF, CAF, THC, VGM, Port Taxes, ETS).
Sales Cycle, The sequence of steps from lead generation to closing.
Segment, A group of customers with similar characteristics or needs.
Stakeholder, A person or group affected by or interested in a business decision.
Strategy, A long-term plan to achieve business goals.
Supply Chain, The network involved in producing and delivering goods.
Synergy, The extra value created when parties work together effectively.
T ▾
Tariff Rule, Rate conditions defining how freight/surcharges are applied.
TEU, Twenty-Foot Equivalent Unit; standard container capacity measure.
THC, Terminal Handling Charge for handling containers at terminal.
Transport Mode, Air, Sea, Road or Rail.
Target, The audience or market a business aims to reach.
Terms and Conditions, The rules and commitments governing a commercial relationship.
Trade-off, A compromise where gaining one benefit requires giving up another.
Transaction, A purchase, sale, or exchange of value.
Trust, Confidence between parties that supports business relationships.
U ▾
UN/LOCODE, United Nations Location Code identifying ports/airports.
Upselling, Encouraging a customer to buy a higher-value option.
Urgency, The pressure created by limited time or availability.
User, The person who uses a product, service, or system.
V ▾
VAT %, Value Added Tax percentage applied after duties.
VGM, Verified Gross Mass; certified container weight required before loading.
Volumetric Weight, Calculated weight based on cargo volume, used in air freight rating.
Value Proposition, The clear benefit that makes an offer attractive.
Validation, Confirmation that an idea, proposal, or assumption is correct.
Viability, The likelihood that a project or plan can work successfully.
Volume, The amount of business, sales, or orders handled.
W ▾
Win-Win, A negotiation result where both sides gain value.
Workflow, The sequence of steps used to complete a business process.
X ▾
XML, Structured code used to exchange H1 declaration data (parties, goods, costs, customs value, duty and VAT).
Before the call starts

Introduce yourself before you begin

Before starting your negotiation activity, please introduce yourself: state your full name, then enter your Institute Code and say your full name clearly again before beginning the simulation.

If a student doesn't say their full name, Negotiation Model will not register their negotiation session, no name, no report, no grade. Remind them every time.
